Why only four attack types?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The CICIDS2017 dataset contains more attack classes, but our cleaned subset
(Tuesday + Friday captures) only contains four distinct attack types that
appear with sufficient flow counts for supervised learning:

1. PortScan (158,804 flows) - network reconnaissance
2. DDoS (128,025 flows) - volumetric flood against port 80
3. FTP-Patator (7,935 flows) - brute force against FTP on port 21
4. SSH-Patator (5,897 flows) - brute force against SSH on port 22

Other CICIDS2017 classes (Bot, Web Attack, Infiltration, DoS GoldenEye,
DoS Hulk, DoS Slowhttptest, DoS Slowloris, Heartbleed) are either:
- Not present in the Tuesday/Friday captures we used
- Have too few flows (<1000) for reliable multi-class training
- Were filtered out during cleaning (NaN/inf removal)

We chose a clean, balanced subset over forcing all classes with sparse data.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why are the scores so high?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three factors inflate the baseline metrics:

1. **Distinctive flow signatures in CICIDS2017**: Each attack type has
   nearly deterministic feature patterns (PortScan = many ports, few packets;
   DDoS = high volume to single port; Patators = repeated SYN to 21/22).
   The dataset is synthetic/controlled, not noisy real traffic.

2. **Destination Port leakage (when included)**: Port 21→FTP-Patator,
   22→SSH-Patator, 80→DDoS. Our headline numbers EXCLUDE this feature
   (78 features), but even without it, packet sizes, timing, and flags
   remain highly discriminative for these specific attacks.

3. **Supervised learning on seen attacks**: The model memorizes CICIDS2017
   patterns. The honest evaluation (chronological split, leave-one-attack-out,
   cross-capture) reveals the true generalization:
   - Chronological: F1 0.998 (still high, temporal consistency)
   - LOAO: 0.04%–45% recall on unseen attack types
   - Cross-capture: 0.04%–2.5% recall on held-out days

The high baseline scores are real for *these attacks in this dataset* but
do not imply production readiness against novel threats.
